# Writing for readers

Treat public-facing documents—including the README, paper, abstracts, and
presentations—as finished works for readers who do not know the project's
development history. Explain the method, contribution, evidence, limitations,
and usage directly, with a coherent narrative about the work itself.

Do not narrate internal iterations, abandoned approaches, implementation
replacements, or what was added, removed, fixed, or retired merely because those
changes occurred during development. Do not carry conversational context or
experiment-log commentary into public-facing prose. Record development history
in dedicated internal notes or changelogs when needed.

Include comparisons, ablations, provenance, and compatibility information when
they serve a scientific argument or a concrete reader need. The distinction is
reader relevance, not whether a statement mentions an earlier method. Before
delivering public-facing writing, read it as a first-time external reader and
remove sentences whose only purpose is to report our editing history.
